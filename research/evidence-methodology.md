# Evidence Methodology

## Evidence Hierarchy (general guidance)
1. **High:** Systematic reviews/meta-analyses of RCTs; high-quality RCTs; recognized clinical guidelines (with evidence grading)
2. **Moderate:** Lower-quality RCTs, well-conducted cohort/case-control studies
3. **Low:** Observational studies with serious limitations, mechanistic studies
4. **Very low/Preclinical:** Animal/laboratory studies, in silico, case reports/series — hypothesis-generating only
5. **Uncertain/Unverified:** Claims without accessible primary source

## Study Appraisal
Use `templates/study-appraisal.md`. Consider:
- Study design, population, sample size, duration/follow-up
- Intervention/comparator, randomization/blinding (if applicable)
- Outcomes (primary/secondary), effect sizes, confidence intervals
- Bias (selection/performance/detection/attrition/reporting)
- Conflicts of interest/funding
- Generalizability/applicability
- Adverse effects reported

## Certainty of Evidence
Report certainty where feasible (e.g., GRADE-like): high/moderate/low/very low. State reasons for downgrading/upgrading.

## Human Applicability
- Separate **on-treatment** effects from **medication-free remission** and **durable disease modification**.
- Distinguish **statistical significance** from **clinical significance**.
- Consider population differences (ethnicity, BMI, age, comorbidities, duration of diabetes).
- Note relapse rates and durability of effect.

## Conflicts of Interest
Record funding/sponsor, author COI if apparent, and potential industry influence. Be transparent about limitations.

## Evidence Update Policy
- **Verification:** Re-check registry/trial status when referenced as recruiting/ongoing; record `last-verified` date.
- **Staleness:** Flag sources >5-10 years old if guidelines/trial landscape may have changed (context-dependent).
- **Contradictions:** Add conflicting evidence to claim ledger when found.
- **Hypotheses:** Clearly label project inferences vs published findings.
- **Unverified claims:** Do not remove uncertainty — flag explicitly.

## Reporting Principles
- Report null/negative results.
- Avoid absolute claims; use calibrated language.
- Never infer individual suitability from population data.
- Do not advise changing prescribed treatment.
- If claim cannot be verified, mark as **UNVERIFIED** and do not fabricate citations.
